WHY THESE NOTES EXIST

A portfolio should show more than finished work. This page tracks the messy middle: what I tried, where I worked with others, and what I’m still getting better at.

2026OCT01 / COLLABORATION

Building under a deadline.

Hackathon experiences in Paris pushed me to move from a promising idea to a demonstrable prototype in a short time.

While working on the AgentGate concept, the difficult part wasn't only technical. It was deciding what was essential to demonstrate, dividing responsibilities, and explaining why the problem mattered to someone hearing about it for the first time.

WHAT CHANGED

I'm paying more attention to scope, communication, and how to validate a claim, not simply how quickly I can write a feature.

2026SEP02 / NEW ENVIRONMENT

A different classroom.

Starting the Digital Innovation Track at EPITECH Paris meant adapting to a new approach to learning.

Working through programming exercises and web assignments in a new environment made it obvious that knowing a concept and being able to build something independently are two different skills.

WHAT CHANGED

I've started treating each assignment as a chance to make small design decisions, document them, and ask what I would improve on the second pass.

2025–26ONGOING03 / EXPERIMENTS

From theory to input and output.

Computer vision and interactive web projects taught me how many decisions sit between an idea and a useful result.

A camera feed introduces timing and imperfect predictions; an interactive 3D page introduces questions about performance and navigation. They look unrelated, but both reward testing with real inputs.

WHAT CHANGED

I want to test earlier, make assumptions explicit, and understand the behavior of the complete system.
